# Club Merch Quotes: Research (IS117-007, group)

A quote-request site for student clubs that need custom shirts for an event: the officer sends the order details, gets an itemized estimate and a receipt, and the shop finds the request. **Working title; name not chosen yet.** Fictional business: no real printing, payments or customers.

**Status:** first draft written by Claude (an AI assistant) on 2026-11-16. Not yet checked by a team member. Business idea chosen by Minh on 2026-11-16 from three AI-suggested options; the team's reason is not recorded yet.

## The question

When a student club needs shirts for an event, what does the officer ordering them need to know before committing, and what do the big online printers leave for a small quote-request site to do better?

## Finding

All three printers answer "how much?" and "when?" for someone ready to design and buy. Custom Ink and ooShirts publish what changes the price (quantity, print colors per side, rush fees) and give dated delivery. Bonfire removes minimums and offers fundraisers where the club pays nothing upfront.

What the club side adds is a step before buying: at least one university (Boise State) has student groups get a vendor's quote approved **before** anything is printed, starting four weeks ahead. On the pages opened, no printer shaped its quote around that approval step. That is a gap to test, not a proven need.

**Evidence vs assumptions:** the Observation columns, prices and dates are what each page showed on 2026-11-16 (evidence). "What it suggests", brand readings, positions and personas are interpretations or hypotheses (assumptions). Nobody has interviewed a club officer.

## Two positions

| | Position A: the quote you can take to approval | Position B: members buy their own |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who it serves | An officer spending club funds who needs a written estimate and a date check first | A fundraising chair who doesn't want the club to pay upfront |
| Why they would choose it | Itemized demo estimate, event-date check, a summary to attach to a funding request | Each member orders and pays for their own size |
| Who already owns it | Nobody, on the pages opened (Custom Ink's purchase-order options not checked) | Bonfire: "No upfront costs", ships to each buyer, pays out profits |
| Evidence | Price factors and delivery windows above; Boise State's quote-then-approve steps | Bonfire's fundraising page |
| Risk | Approval rules differ by school and NJIT's are unknown; a demo can't print or approve anything | Needs real payments (deferred by the class) and faces a free, established competitor |

## Design, brand character and honest persuasion (proposals)

AI suggestions drawn from the observations above, not team decisions.

- **Design:** all three are friendly, rounded, catalog-first. A planning tool can look calmer and more like a form: date and estimate up front, no product grid.
- **Brand character:** to be chosen by the team. One option is a Sage with a Caregiver streak: it explains the price and timing so the officer looks prepared.
- **Honest persuasion:**
  - *Reciprocity:* show the date check and the price factors for free, before asking for any contact details.
  - *Commitment:* the officer saves a request with a reference number and comes back to confirm it once funding is approved, a step they chose to start.
- **Avoid:** reviews, "trusted by" numbers, guarantees or delivery dates we can't back. ooShirts' empty review quotes show how filler looks.
